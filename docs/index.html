ember

A judgment-free space to talk things through.

Ember is a private AI companion for your wellbeing. It lives in your Mac's menu bar, remembers what matters, and helps you connect your thoughts, feelings and what you do.

Download for Mac Free · Apple silicon · macOS 26 or later

What Ember does

Talks it through

Type or talk out loud. Ember listens, asks one good question at a time, and uses techniques from CBT and ACT.

Understands your day

With your permission it notices which apps and pages you're in, so it can answer "what did I do this morning?" and spot patterns.

Checks in

A short spoken check-in at sensible moments: a morning intention, a nudge when you drift, a wind-down at night. Never during calls.

Plans in small steps

Tell it about a deadline and it breaks the work into daily steps in your calendar, only after you approve.

Help when it's hard

One tap for an urge, overwhelm, anxiety or a low moment, with short breathing and grounding exercises.

Your fire

A living flame that grows with how your day is going, and dims, never out, after late nights and long drifts.

Private by design

No account

Nothing to sign up for. Ember has no servers.

Runs on your Mac

The AI model, your conversations and what Ember learns about your day stay on your Mac.

Encrypted

Ember's database is encrypted on disk, with the key in your Keychain. Delete everything any time.

Install

  1. Download Ember-<version>.dmg from the latest release, open it and drag Ember to Applications.
  2. Open Ember. The first time, macOS says it can't check the app for malicious software: click Done.
  3. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click Open Anyway next to Ember.
  4. From then on Ember opens like any other app, and keeps itself up to date (it asks first).

Ember's own AI model is coming soon. Until then, Ember runs a model in LM Studio: install it, download a model (Gemma 4 works well), start its server, and pick it when Ember asks.

Ember is an AI, not a therapist or a crisis service. If you're in crisis, contact your local emergency number or find a free helpline at findahelpline.com.
